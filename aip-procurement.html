AIP procurement: the key steps, sealed bids, and grant timing

How an airport sponsor buys with Airport Improvement Program (AIP) funds, in the order it usually happens, and the rules that decide each step. Written from the FAA’s AIP Handbook (Order 5100.38D, Change 1) and 2 CFR Part 200.

The short version

  • Timing decides eligibility. Project costs must be incurred after the grant is executed unless the law allows otherwise. Entitlement funds, and a few other cases set by statute, are exceptions.
  • Bids usually come before the application. The FAA expects the application to carry the actual bid amount, so sponsors usually advertise and open bids first.
  • Above the simplified acquisition threshold, tell the ADO. Written notice before award is required in three situations, covered below.
  • Local rules apply too. Your own state and local procurement procedures sit alongside the federal standards.
  • Build the file as you go. The independent estimate, bid tabulation, price analysis and signed cost statement are what the ADO uses to judge cost reasonableness.

The key steps

AIP-funded purchases follow a common order. Table 5-4 of the Handbook lists the key steps, and these are the ones that shape a purchase.

A selection from Table 5-4, in the order the Handbook lists them. The Handbook also lists earlier steps such as environmental review documents, final specifications and a DBE plan. It is not a mandated sequence, so confirm your schedule with your ADO.

Grant timing: when costs become eligible

Under 49 USC 47110(b)(2), project costs must be incurred after the grant is executed unless the law specifically allows otherwise. That rule is why the order of the steps matters.

Entitlement funds have an exception. Costs paid with passenger, cargo or nonprimary entitlement funds may be reimbursed even if incurred before the grant is executed, as long as all other applicable AIP requirements have been met. For discretionary and state apportionment funds, the exceptions are narrow and set by statute.

In practice, sponsors advertise and open bids first, then apply. The Handbook states FAA policy that the application incorporates the actual bid or negotiated amount, and describes an application built on estimates as suboptimal. In the Handbook’s common sequence, the contract is awarded after the grant offer is accepted.

Early indications from your ADO are for planning only. Whether and when to start is the sponsor’s decision.

The federal share and the ceiling

The grant agreement sets the maximum federal cost, and total allowable federal costs cannot exceed it except as the amendment rules allow. The normal federal share for a small hub is 90% of allowable project costs. The FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024 added a temporary 95% share for nonhub and nonprimary airports in fiscal years 2025 and 2026; small hubs stay at 90%.

Sealed bids under 2 CFR 200.320

Sealed bidding, called formal advertising in the Handbook edition, is the standard method for larger purchases. Bids are publicly solicited and publicly opened at the time and place in the invitation, and a firm fixed price contract is awarded in writing to the lowest responsive and responsible bidder. It works when three things are true:

  • There is a complete, adequate and realistic specification.
  • Two or more responsible bidders are willing and able to compete.
  • The purchase suits a firm fixed price contract, and selection can be made principally on price.

Notify the ADO before award

If the purchase is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold, you must notify the ADO in writing before award in three situations: the award will be made without competition, only one bid is received, or the award will go to other than the apparent low bidder. The ADO also has the option to conduct a pre-award review in situations like these, or, above that threshold, when a brand-name product is specified.

Responsive, responsible, and apparent low

The apparent low bidder is simply the lowest-priced bid, before any finding on whether it is responsive or responsible. A responsible bidder can perform successfully, considering integrity, compliance with public policy, past performance, and financial and technical resources. If you reject a bid, you must document and justify the reason.

Say how the winner will be chosen

Bid documents must state how the successful bid will be determined. That can include bid alternates and the availability of federal funding. With alternates, the solicitation must set the base bid and the order of the alternates based on available funding.

Keep the specification open

Requirements that narrow the field can reduce the number of bidders, which undermines the method itself. The Handbook’s example is specifying snow removal equipment built to highway-vehicle standards.

Thresholds

Two federal thresholds set how much of the process applies to a purchase.

ThresholdAmountWhere it matters here
Micro-purchase$15,000The federal micro-purchase threshold. A recipient may self-certify a higher one, up to $50,000, under 2 CFR 200.320(a)(1)
Simplified acquisition$350,000Between the micro-purchase threshold and this one, small-purchase procedures are allowed. Above it: sealed bids or proposals, a cost or price analysis, and written notice to the ADO before award in the three situations above

The current Handbook (Order 5100.38D, Change 1) still shows $150,000 for the simplified acquisition threshold, citing the older 2 CFR 200.88. The current federal figure is $350,000.

Cost reasonableness

Under 2 CFR 200.324, you make an independent estimate before receiving bids, and above the simplified acquisition threshold you perform a cost or price analysis. For equipment purchases, Table 3-67 of the Handbook lists what the file contains:

  • Two or more sealed bids: a price analysis (above the simplified acquisition threshold), the engineer’s estimate, a signed sponsor statement that the cost is reasonable and, where one was required, that a price analysis was performed, and the bid tabulation.
  • A single bidder: a cost analysis, the engineer’s estimate, a signed statement recommending the FAA accept it, and the bid tabulation.

What goes in the bid package

  • The FAA’s required contract provisions. 2 CFR 200.327 and Appendix II to Part 200 require the applicable provisions in the sponsor’s contracts. For AIP, that means the FAA’s required provisions go into the bid package. The Handbook edition reproduces this section as 200.326.
  • A Buy American certificate. 49 USC 50101 sets the rule. Per Handbook Table X-1, the sponsor either certifies that all products are wholly produced in the United States from 100% U.S. materials, certifies that the equipment is on the Nationwide Buy American conformance list, or requests a waiver.
  • Written protest procedures. By FAA policy they must be in place before an AIP-funded procurement starts, and the ADO gets a copy of any protest and its resolution.
  • Your local procedures. 2 CFR 200.318(a) requires documented procedures that align with state, local and tribal law as well as the federal standards.

Common questions

Can we advertise for bids before the grant is executed?

Yes. Advertising and opening bids come before the grant application in the Handbook’s common steps, and the contract is awarded after the grant offer is accepted. Project costs incurred before the grant is executed are generally not eligible, with an exception for entitlement funds and a few other cases set by statute.

Does the ADO have to sign off on our award?

No. The award is the sponsor’s decision. Above the simplified acquisition threshold, you must notify the ADO in writing before award if the award is made without competition, only one bid is received, or the award goes to other than the apparent low bidder. The ADO also has the option to conduct a pre-award review in situations like these.

What if every bid comes in over the estimate?

The Handbook does not prescribe a single answer. If you open bids before applying, it directs the ADO to issue the grant based on the actual bid amounts, not the programmed amount, so raise the gap with your ADO early. After the grant is executed, the agreement caps the federal share except as the amendment rules allow. The sponsor must have the non-federal funds for its share (Grant Assurance 3). You can also reject all bids with a documented reason and re-advertise. To prepare for this before you advertise, write the solicitation with a base bid and alternates ordered by available funding, as Table U-8 describes.

Is the limit $350,000 or my airport’s own number?

The federal simplified acquisition threshold is $350,000. A recipient may adopt a lower threshold in its own policy, and when it does, the airport’s lower figure governs its own procedures. Ask your ADO which figure it applies to the written-notice rule.

Do our state and local procurement rules still apply?

Yes. 2 CFR 200.318(a) requires documented procurement procedures that align with state, local and tribal law and with the federal standards. In practice an airport follows both.
