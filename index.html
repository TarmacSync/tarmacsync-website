What is Airport Procurement Intelligence?

Airport Procurement Intelligence connects project facts, airport policy, funding requirements, available contract pathways, governed guidance, decisions, and evidence so airport teams can understand how a procurement or project may move forward.

ERP / financial system

Records the transaction after procurement decisions are made.

Bid or solicitation portal

Runs the solicitation once the procurement method is selected.

Document storage

Holds files but not the reasoning, gaps, or open decisions.

TarmacSync

Connects policy, funding, FAA guidance, contracts, evidence, and next actions — before and across those systems.

Questions we're often asked.

Who is the Founding Airport Partner Program for?

U.S. public-use airports or sponsors — especially GA, reliever, non-hub, and small-hub airports. Teams with a real upcoming procurement or project who want to help shape an airport-specific tool.

What does the 90-day partnership include?

A 45-minute kickoff, guided setup, two representative workflows, two structured feedback sessions, and direct founder access. No fee. No automatic renewal. No purchase commitment.

What information would our airport need to provide?

Non-sensitive materials only: your procurement policy, purchasing thresholds, and background on one or two representative procurements or projects. No SSI, CUI, classified, or protected security information.

Does TarmacSync replace our ERP or eProcurement system?

No. TarmacSync is the intelligence layer that helps your team evaluate the path before a requisition or solicitation begins. Your existing systems remain in place.

How does TarmacSync use FAA and airport-specific sources?

TarmacSync connects purchase decisions with relevant FAA guidance, Grant Assurances, 2 CFR Part 200, and other governed sources — with citations retrieved from official documents. It does not replace FAA or legal review.

Is airport information used to train public AI models?

TarmacSync does not use airport information to train public AI models. Approved service providers may process information to operate the service under disclosed data-handling terms.
